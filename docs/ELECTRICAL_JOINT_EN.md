# Electrical wiring and joint travel checks

### View and select parts by role

The 3D viewport has **All**, **Electrical only**, **Structure only**, and **Transmission only** buttons. A role button displays and selects only parts of that role. It does not bring in other parts from a mixed-role group. Select a part and use **Part role / default color…** to assign or change its role.

The role view temporarily shows canonical colors to make parts easier to identify. **All** restores saved or custom part colors and the previous manually hidden state. Use the existing **Show all** command when you intend to reveal every hidden part. For legacy parts without role metadata, the app recognizes only an exact canonical role color or a CAD part ID explicitly linked to an electrical circuit. It does not infer roles from names. A yellow part or electrical role does not create a circuit component.

### Build and inspect a circuit

Open **Drawing / analysis → Electrical circuit / wiring / voltage drop**. Add a battery, wire, switch, resistor, generic load, motor or MCU/board power load. Enter the battery's **Battery + / supply node** and **Battery - / return node**; give connected terminals the same node name. `GND` is the voltage reference. For example: battery `VPLUS`–`GND`, switch `VPLUS`–`SWITCHED`, wire `SWITCHED`–`MOTORPLUS`, motor `MOTORPLUS`–`GND`. Node names accept letters, digits, `_`, and `-`.

Enter ratings and wire dimensions from the actual product data. **Power on / enable battery output** controls whether the battery supplies its circuit path; turning it off retains the entered battery voltage and terminal names. You can also select a battery in the circuit list and use its power on/off control. Select **Recalculate circuit** to inspect the supply path to a load, its return path, node voltages, branch currents and warnings. Then **Save to design**. A circuit item can be linked to a CAD part for identification; this does not create routed 3D wires or drive a mechanical joint. The illustrative example replaces an existing circuit only after confirmation.

Select **View schematic…** in the electrical editor to open **Electrical schematic / wiring connections** in a separate window. The left pane shows actual component terminals and nodes; the right pane shows the selected MCU connector pins. Use **Select MCU / pin connections…** or click a physical pin to select a board and target terminal. Save the pin editor, save schematic changes, then **Save to design** in the electrical editor to commit the changes. Canceling a dialog discards that dialog's edits. Use **Fit all**, **Zoom in**, and **Zoom out** to inspect the circuit. Crossing lines connect only where a junction is marked. See the [MCU pin connection guide and coverage](MCU_PIN_CONNECTIONS_EN.md).

The calculation is a **DC resistive operating-point estimate**. Wire resistance uses entered resistivity, length and conductor area. The initial resistivity approximates room-temperature copper. A motor's rated voltage and current define one equivalent resistance. An explicitly entered startup current gives a separate startup estimate, not a startup waveform. Results show node voltages, branch currents, voltage differences, power and warnings for entered current limits, MCU polarity and voltage more than 10% from a load's rating. Product datasheet limits still need review. Unsolvable wiring may be saved as an *unverified draft* after confirmation.

### Open wires and MCU connectivity

Uncheck **Wire connected** in a wire item to model an open circuit. Its endpoints and node names stay in the project, but it no longer conducts current. Previously saved wires remain connected by default. In an MCU item, terminal A is **VCC** and terminal B is **GND/return**. The checker distinguishes a path to the DC supply, a return path, and the entered voltage relative to the MCU's rated value. A node merely named `GND` is the voltage reference; model the actual return wiring too.

Uncheck **Power on / enable battery output** to disconnect a battery from the supply path without changing its entered voltage. With that source off, check the resulting current and MCU supply/return results. An off source is not a zero-volt product specification.

Use **Select MCU / pin connections…** to select an exact board, physical connector pin and component terminal or another MCU pin. Existing custom pins remain distinguishable from official pins and are preserved. The original **MCU signal pin = node** text editor is also retained. The check examines passive continuity through modeled connected wires and closed switches to another circuit terminal. Unlisted pins are not checked. Documented logic-voltage differences and direct GPIO connections to power or motors produce warnings; the app does not calculate actual GPIO limits or level-translator behavior. PASS, WARN and FAIL refer only to the entered wiring model and ratings. No board fault is measured automatically; the checker does not establish GPIO direction, pull resistors, communication protocols or firmware behavior.

The component editor's **Find model…** searches an offline, source-linked parts catalog. **Open official source** lets you review the original specification. Only a specific supported model can prefill its name, source and clearly documented values. Family entries and components the DC solver does not model, including diodes, motor drivers and sensors, are reference-only. This is not live web scraping; check the source for revisions, ordering variants and test conditions. Raspberry Pi 3 Model B+ and 4 Model B are computer boards represented here only as DC loads. Their documented 5 V input may be prefilled, but you must enter a measured or otherwise known board operating current. A 2.5 A or 3 A *recommended power supply* is not the board's consumption.

The bundled references cover representative Raspberry Pi 3 B+/4/5/Zero/Pico, Arduino UNO/Nano/Mega, STM32 Nucleo/Discovery boards and chip families, ESP32, encoders, motors, drivers, wire, resistors, capacitors, MOSFETs, diodes, connectors, fuses and supplies. This is not an exhaustive list of every manufacturer SKU. If the exact part is absent, use its official selector and enter verified operating parameters manually. A partial name such as “STM32 B…” is not enough to identify a board.

For motion, open **Assembly → Drive joint / check interference**, select a movable joint and adjust its angle or position. The app checks solid intersections from the current to the proposed pose at intervals of at most **2° or 0.5 mm** and blocks a detected collision. **Check both travel limits** surveys the selected independent axis from the current pose to its negative and positive configured limits. It shows the first colliding sample and the preceding clear sample. This read-only check does not change joint limits. A linked or passive loop axis cannot be selected as an independent drive. The physical spur-gear command now gives the input joint up to ±360° when the linked output range permits it.

These sampled checks cannot certify continuous collision-free travel between samples or account for manufacturing tolerances, elastic deflection and applied loads. The electrical workspace does not yet simulate PCB routing, battery protection/discharge, motor torque and dynamics, firmware, transients or full SPICE circuits. Electrical loads do not automatically actuate CAD joints.
